# Vatsalya Bhawan redesign

The new homepage is in `src/VatsalyaBhawan.js`, with styling in `src/index.css` and shared booking validation in `src/booking.js`. Run `npm start`, `npm run build`, or `CI=true npm test -- --watchAll=false`.

## Identity and imagery

- The brand uses the real Devanagari initial “व” as a minimal vector mark, paired with Lora for English display type and Manrope for body text. The glyph is outlined from Noto Sans Devanagari to avoid font-loading changes in the logo.
- `public/assets/ayodhya-aerial.jpg` is a retained AI-generated artist impression. It is not used as the endpoint of the current satellite film.
- The hero is a 21.5-second journey from Earth through India and real Ayodhya satellite geography, then into an AI aerial interpretation of the city and Ram Mandir. The first 10 seconds reuse the registered NASA/Copernicus camera zoom. Two Veo clips were made with explicit start/end reference frames; the actual final frame of the first generated clip starts the next. The second clip is trimmed before a visible generation defect and eases into a temple-focused hold. The generated buildings and landscape are artistic interpretations, not documentary drone footage or surveyed geometry. References, exact image prompts, selected frames, and assembly instructions are in `scripts/hero-film/TEMPLE_NOTES.md`.
- Four Hindi/English captions follow actual media time: “जम्बूद्वीपे / Jambudvīpe” at 0 seconds, “भारतखण्डे / Bhāratakhaṇḍe” at 2.4, “आर्यावर्ते / Āryāvarte” at 4.4, and “अयोध्या / Ayodhyā” at 6.4. At 20 seconds, once the temple view settles, the logo, navigation, headline and actions fade in over 0.9 seconds with the background shading. Scrolling, reduced motion, blocked autoplay and failed media reveal the normal page immediately. The film is silent, plays once, and provides pause/replay controls. Desktop and portrait versions share timing; the portrait framing follows the temple. Opening and arrival posters come from each encoded film so completion does not switch to a different composition. Compact footer credits identify the AI interpretations and geographic/architectural references.
- “Watch the journey” provides an explicit restart, including recovery after blocked autoplay. Completed films reveal the matching arrival poster so a preference change cannot leave a paused globe over the normal interface.
- The everyday palette uses Saryu blue ink (`#123C51`), clean ivory (`#FFFCF5`), aqua (`#DFF0EF`) and sandstone (`#FCE7C4`). Flat vermilion buttons (`#BB3D21`) retain softly arched corners and white diya accents. Marigold (`#F2B638`) is reserved for suns, flames and decorative stars. Text and button colour pairs exceed 4.5:1 contrast. The optional “Diwali lights” toggle remembers the guest's choice locally and adds static warm lamp glows. Festival decoration stays hidden during the opening film; reduced motion remains respected.
- Six original code-based SVG illustrations in `public/assets/` bring local architectural forms to the text sections: a Saryu riverfront panorama, four distinct landmark motifs and a small diya composition. They are decorative artwork, not photographs or navigation maps; the real street map remains separate. Each SVG is self-contained without fonts, scripts or external references. The welcome facade sits inside an aqua arch with a marigold sun, while the shorter closing enquiry section pairs “Stay in Ayodhya.” with the riverfront art.
- The welcome section includes an original devotional illustration of Shri Ram with a long flowing saffron angavastra, alongside the property introduction. `public/assets/shri-ram-angavastra.webp` was generated and refined with the built-in image-generation tool, then encoded to a 190KB transparent WebP without resizing. The full figure and cloth remain visible at desktop and phone sizes; it is not a photograph, booking badge or background watermark. The exact generation and refinement prompts are saved in `public/assets/shri-ram-angavastra-prompt.txt`. A small static code-based saffron drape above the closing riverfront artwork echoes the cloth without adding another deity image or extra copy.
- The city guide uses actual OpenStreetMap street, lane, footpath and water geometry, with geographically placed landmarks and Vatsalya Bhawan. The map supports zooming and panning; choosing a landmark offers Google Maps walking directions from the bhawan or the guest's current location. The downloadable PDF has a city overview and a closer central street map, with the bhawan's contact details alongside. No straight-line routes or guessed journey times are presented. Google Maps handles live routing, while temple entry arrangements remain subject to local controls. Map source data, attribution and regeneration instructions are in `scripts/map-guide/`.
- The guide adds four compact native disclosures for Ram Mandir, Hanuman Garhi, Kanak Bhawan and Ram ki Paidi. The latter three descriptions come from the District Ayodhya tourist-place pages; Ram Mandir's description is supported by the Press Information Bureau's “The Story of Ram Mandir” (24 November 2025). Each place links to Google Maps and the temple trust or district guide. No opening hours, tickets or journey times are invented. Visible place headings, updated metadata, a no-JavaScript map link and `public/sitemap.xml` support discovery without a separate content framework.
- `public/assets/exterior-refined.jpg` is an AI-reframed architectural view based on the actual exterior photo, using `exterior-v5-clean.png`. The camera perspective is level and compressed, with the ground floor and two balcony levels, shutter, entry, blue columns and warm lighting retained. Wires were cleaned up. This JPEG is retained as the source for the transparent cutout. Fine details remain reconstructed; the original photograph is available on the page.
- The welcome section now uses `public/assets/exterior-cutout.png`, a transparent RGBA extraction of that refined facade. Sky, neighbouring buildings and road were removed; the rooftop, balconies, entrance, gates and steps remain. It displays at native proportions inside the decorative arch with a subtle CSS shadow. The JPEG remains as the source variant. The cutout was made with the built-in ImageGen tool; its prompt is saved in `public/assets/exterior-cutout-prompt.txt`.

The deluxe view is a tighter foot-of-bed composition; family and standard use level views with straighter verticals. Natural framing crops peripheral amenities rather than forcing an ultrawide field of view.

## Enquiries

The form prepares an encoded WhatsApp message. It neither sends a message nor claims a booking has been received. The guest reviews the text, opens WhatsApp, and sends it themselves; the hotel confirms availability and pricing. No payment or live inventory integration is implied.

The homepage puts room choices and the gallery before the city guide. Each room displays its existing occupancy guidance and amenities, a direct enquiry action and separate photo details. Selecting a room preserves the guest's dates and group size; larger groups receive a multiple-room note. Moving arrival beyond the selected departure advances departure by one day, while later departures are preserved. The review and WhatsApp link share one message builder.

The gallery exposes eight original photos in Rooms, Around the bhawan and Bathrooms, including all three room types. The viewer and arrow keys stay within the selected category. Room details retain the AI-reframed/original comparison. Native dialogs, 44px controls and explicit review-heading focus support touch and keyboard use.

The redesign is published separately at https://eye-52.github.io/vatsalya-bhawan/ from EYE-52/vatsalya-bhawan. The original RT-1904129 repository and website are preserved. Public asset paths use a relative deployment base; `npm run deploy` targets the new repository explicitly.
