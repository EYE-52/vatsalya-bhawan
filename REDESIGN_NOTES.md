# Vatsalya Bhawan redesign

The new homepage is in `src/VatsalyaBhawan.js`, with styling in `src/index.css` and shared booking validation in `src/booking.js`. Run `npm start`, `npm run build`, or `CI=true npm test -- --watchAll=false`.

## Identity and imagery

- The brand uses the real Devanagari initial “व” as a minimal vector mark, paired with Lora for English display type and Manrope for body text. The glyph is outlined from Noto Sans Devanagari to avoid font-loading changes in the logo.
- `public/assets/ayodhya-aerial.jpg` is a retained AI-generated artist impression. It is not used as the endpoint of the current satellite film.
- The hero is a **10-second** journey (240 frames at 24 fps) from Earth through India and real Ayodhya satellite geography to an angled AI interpretation of Ram Mandir’s completed shikhar and saffron flag. The geographic opening uses 84 frames (3.5 seconds), followed by a single reference-guided descent compressed into 120 frames (5 seconds), then a 36-frame stationary hold (1.5 seconds). Retiming preserves the actual first and last source frames; there is no additional optical zoom that could crop the flag. The generated scene is an artistic interpretation, not documentary drone footage or surveyed geometry. Architectural guidance comes from the Prime Minister’s Office/PIB photograph of the completed mandapa and shikhara on 25 November 2025, licensed under GODL-India: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shri_Ram_Janambhoomi_Mandir_Mandapa_and_Sikhara.jpg. Reference and assembly provenance remains in `scripts/hero-film/TEMPLE_NOTES.md`.
- Four Hindi/English captions follow media time: “जम्बूद्वीपे / Jambudvīpe” at 0 seconds, “भारतखण्डे / Bhāratakhaṇḍe” at 0.9, “आर्यावर्ते / Āryāvarte” at 1.8, and “अयोध्या नगरी / Ayodhya Nagari” at 2.8. The arrival threshold is frame 204 (8.5 seconds). The header, navigation, headline, description and booking actions remain visible and interactive from the first frame; only the journey caption changes, in a fixed-height eyebrow slot directly above the headline. It replaces the ordinary hero introduction during playback and does not cover the temple or shift the layout. The mobile hero has a 740px minimum height to avoid overlap on short phone screens. A readable gradient stays over the film throughout. Scrolling hides the caption; reduced motion, blocked autoplay and failed media show the arrival poster immediately. The approach is silent and plays once. At 8.5 seconds it switches to a 7.708-second loop of the saffron flag fluttering over the stationary shikhar, before the fallback still hold. Pause/resume affects the active video, while “Watch the journey” replays the whole approach. The loop is preloaded, pauses offscreen and falls back to the matching poster if playback fails. Desktop and portrait versions share timing with a fixed portrait crop centred at 45% of the landscape width during the generated descent and flag loop, keeping the fully extended flag inside the phone frame. Opening and arrival posters come from the encoded films. Public media uses the `ayodhya-shikhar-journey-v2` stem. The film and matching poster begin below the header with top alignment to protect the flag at desktop and phone sizes.
- “Watch the journey” provides an explicit restart, including recovery after blocked autoplay. Completed approaches reveal the matching flag loop, or the arrival poster for reduced motion and media failure, so a preference change cannot leave a paused globe over the normal interface.
- The everyday palette uses Saryu blue ink (`#123C51`), clean ivory (`#FFFCF5`), aqua (`#DFF0EF`) and sandstone (`#FCE7C4`), with vermilion buttons (`#BB3D21`). The optional **“Diwali mode”** toggle remembers the guest’s choice locally and switches back through **“Everyday Ayodhya”**. Its coordinated evening palette uses deep blue (`#14263E`), warm ivory (`#FFF5DC`), marigold gold (`#F5BE50`) and saffron/vermilion accents. Navigation, hero shading, gold buttons with dark text, the warm welcome arch, closing enquiry and footer form one theme. Booking surfaces stay light; room photographs, platform logos and geographic map colours retain their original appearance. Static lamps remain an accent, with no confetti or added animation. Festival mode remains visible while the film plays, and reduced motion is respected.
- `public/assets/ayodhya-deepotsav-art.svg` adapts the existing riverfront vector architecture into a night scene with a crescent moon, rows of diyas and water reflections; it replaces `ayodhya-river-art.svg` in the closing enquiry only during Diwali mode. `src/assets/diwali-rangoli.svg` adds a restrained welcome motif. Both are code-native decorative artwork, self-contained and without external resources; they are not documentary imagery.
- Six original code-based SVG illustrations in `public/assets/` bring local architectural forms to the text sections: a Saryu riverfront panorama, four distinct landmark motifs and a small diya composition. They are decorative artwork, not photographs or navigation maps; the real street map remains separate. Each SVG is self-contained without fonts, scripts or external references. The welcome facade sits inside an aqua arch with a marigold sun, while the shorter closing enquiry section pairs “Stay in Ayodhya.” with the riverfront art.
- The welcome section includes an original devotional illustration of Shri Ram with a long flowing saffron angavastra, alongside the property introduction. `public/assets/shri-ram-angavastra.webp` was generated and refined with the built-in image-generation tool, then encoded to a 190KB transparent WebP without resizing. The full figure and cloth remain visible at desktop and phone sizes; it is not a photograph, booking badge or background watermark. The exact generation and refinement prompts are saved in `public/assets/shri-ram-angavastra-prompt.txt`. A small static code-based saffron drape above the closing riverfront artwork echoes the cloth without adding another deity image or extra copy.
- The city guide uses actual OpenStreetMap street, lane, footpath and water geometry, with geographically placed landmarks and Vatsalya Bhawan. The map supports zooming and panning; choosing a landmark offers Google Maps walking directions from the bhawan or the guest's current location. The downloadable PDF has a city overview and a closer central street map, with the bhawan's contact details alongside. No straight-line routes or guessed journey times are presented. Google Maps handles live routing, while temple entry arrangements remain subject to local controls. Map source data, attribution and regeneration instructions are in `scripts/map-guide/`.
- The interactive map spans the full viewport width, with walking actions and a compact hotel panel underneath. A separate, wider OSM snapshot supplies real surrounding streets across the landscape view; it does not change the downloadable PDF. Landmark labels use measured bounds, alternate sides and collision checks while pins retain their geographic positions and 44px touch targets.
- Map buttons use half-level zoom steps with a 0.65-second native Leaflet glide; landmark selection and “Show all” use the same motion. Pinch zoom keeps its exact scale without snapping to preset levels. Reduced-motion preferences disable these animations. The street geometry is projected once into a complete geographic SVG overlay so zooming out can reveal surrounding roads continuously instead of waiting for a viewport redraw.
- The guide adds four compact native disclosures for Ram Mandir, Hanuman Garhi, Kanak Bhawan and Ram ki Paidi. The latter three descriptions come from the District Ayodhya tourist-place pages; Ram Mandir's description is supported by the Press Information Bureau's “The Story of Ram Mandir” (24 November 2025). Each place links to Google Maps and the temple trust or district guide. No opening hours, tickets or journey times are invented. Visible place headings, updated metadata, a no-JavaScript map link and `public/sitemap.xml` support discovery without a separate content framework.
- `public/assets/exterior-refined.jpg` is an AI-reframed architectural view based on the actual exterior photo, using `exterior-v5-clean.png`. The camera perspective is level and compressed, with the ground floor and two balcony levels, shutter, entry, blue columns and warm lighting retained. Wires were cleaned up. This JPEG is retained as the source for the transparent cutout. Fine details remain reconstructed; the original photograph is available on the page.
- The welcome section now uses `public/assets/exterior-cutout.png`, a transparent RGBA extraction of that refined facade. Sky, neighbouring buildings and road were removed; the rooftop, balconies, entrance, gates and steps remain. It displays at native proportions inside the decorative arch with a subtle CSS shadow. The JPEG remains as the source variant. The cutout was made with the built-in ImageGen tool; its prompt is saved in `public/assets/exterior-cutout-prompt.txt`.
- `src/assets/room-refined-{deluxe,family,standard}.jpg` use `deluxe-v4.png`, `family-v3.png` and `standard-v3.png`. They were generated from the actual original room photos. The deluxe view is a tighter foot-of-bed composition; family and standard use level views with straighter verticals. Natural framing crops peripheral amenities rather than forcing an ultrawide field of view.
- These are AI perspective reconstructions. Floral textile details, small shelf objects, fine headboard lines and cooler branding can differ from the originals. Original-photo comparisons remain available. Prominent homepage reconstruction notes were removed at the owner's request; the provenance is retained here and in the footer image credits.
- Source and intermediate PNGs remain in `/Users/divyansh/Projects/Rishabhs/photo-polish/`. Photo generation used the built-in ImageGen tool. Satellite source preparation and provenance are documented in `scripts/hero-film/GEOGRAPHIC_NOTES.md`. The retained satellite-only city approach is documented in `scripts/hero-film/CONTINUOUS_NOTES.md`; the current temple continuation is in `scripts/hero-film/TEMPLE_NOTES.md`. `REVEAL_NOTES.md`, `DRONE_NOTES.md`, `APPROACH_NOTES.md`, `VEO_NOTES.md` and the original renderer's `README.md` document superseded or rejected films; none of the rejected generated transitions is used by the current hero.

## Enquiries

The bottom-right WhatsApp shortcut stays fixed throughout the page. It opens a generic stay enquiry to the property's existing number, +91 94513 38729; the guest sends the message themselves. On phones it becomes a 52px icon and moves above the persistent booking bar. The film control sits on the left to keep both actions accessible.

The form prepares an encoded WhatsApp message. It neither sends a message nor claims a booking has been received. The guest reviews the text, opens WhatsApp, and sends it themselves; the hotel confirms availability and pricing. No payment or live inventory integration is implied.

The homepage puts room choices and the gallery before the city guide. Each room displays its existing occupancy guidance and amenities, a direct enquiry action and separate photo details. Selecting a room preserves the guest's dates and group size; larger groups receive a multiple-room note. Moving arrival beyond the selected departure advances departure by one day, while later departures are preserved. The review and WhatsApp link share one message builder.

The gallery exposes eight original photos in Rooms, Around the bhawan and Bathrooms, including all three room types. The viewer and arrow keys stay within the selected category. Room details retain the AI-reframed/original comparison. Native dialogs, 44px controls and explicit review-heading focus support touch and keyboard use.
